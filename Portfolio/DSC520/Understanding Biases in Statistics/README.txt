Bias and Ethics in Healthcare Research
The Tuskegee Syphilis Study

Author
Shelia Howard

Project overview
This written case study examines how racial assumptions, participant
selection, and institutional decisions shaped the U.S. Public Health
Service syphilis study at Tuskegee. It considers how those choices
affected participant welfare and the validity of the study's conclusions.

Approach
I applied concepts from statistics and research ethics to historical
material cited in the report. A summary table connects confirmation bias,
selection bias, outcome bias, and systemic bias to examples from the
study and explains their implications. The analysis is qualitative.

Key findings
The report argues that unsupported racial assumptions shaped the research
design and that recruitment from a narrow population limited how broadly
the findings could be applied. It also discusses the failure to obtain
informed consent and the withholding of effective treatment. These
decisions show how research priorities can compromise participant welfare
and how unexamined assumptions can weaken scientific conclusions.

How to review
Open "Bias and Ethics in Healthcare Research_The Tuskegee Syphilis Study.pdf".
Read the sections on racial assumptions, external validity, and ethical
judgment, then use Table 1 to compare the four bias categories. The reference
list identifies the CDC historical overview and Mike X Cohen's statistics
text. A PDF reader is the only requirement. No dataset or code is needed.

Scope
The report interprets a documented historical case using published sources.
It does not analyze individual participant records or estimate causal effects.
