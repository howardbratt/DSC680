DSC670 · Advanced Uses of Generative AI

Healthcare Data Mapping Assistant

I built a Streamlit prototype to recommend mappings from incoming healthcare fields to an approved schema of 13 demographic fields. An analyst can enter a name, datatype, description, and an optional sample value. The app returns a recommendation and rationale or routes the field for analyst review. For example, it mapped a birth date to the approved date-of-birth field and flagged a combined full name for review.

After fine-tuning on synthetic examples, the model returned the correct target or review decision in 41 of 48 held-out cases and produced valid JSON in all 48. It missed one required review on a separate eight-case challenge set. The app records the analyst’s decision separately.

DSC680 · Applied Data Science · Project 1

Hospital Readmission Penalty Risk

I analyzed 11,720 hospital and condition records from the FY 2026 CMS Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program to assess whether condition, state, and discharge volume could help flag records for further review. I compared logistic regression and random forest, kept each hospital’s records together during cross-validation, and excluded variables that could reveal the outcome.

Random forest achieved a ROC-AUC of 0.663. At its default threshold, logistic regression identified about 68% of records with an excess readmission ratio above 1.0. These results give hospital quality teams a starting point for deciding where to review readmission performance.

DSC680 · Applied Data Science · Project 2

Bike Demand Forecasting for Operational Planning

I analyzed 17,379 hourly Capital Bikeshare records from 2011 and 2012 to evaluate forecasts for staffing and bike reserve planning. I compared ridge regression and random forest with a baseline based on typical rentals by hour and weekday. Models were trained and validated on earlier dates and tested on a later period.

Adding observed weather reduced mean absolute error by 5.4% for ridge regression and 17.7% for random forest. At 8 a.m., the calendar-only ridge model underestimated demand in 90 of 145 test hours, with an average shortfall of about 292 rentals across those misses. I recommended reviewing the 8 a.m. period and testing on recent data with weather forecasts available at planning time before changing staffing or bike allocations.
